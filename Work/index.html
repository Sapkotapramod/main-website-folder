The physics in plain words

Every object with mass pulls on every other object. The pull is F = G × M × m / (r + h)². G is a tiny fixed number, M and m are the two masses, and (r + h) is the distance between their centres.

Distance matters a lot. Double the distance and the pull becomes four times weaker. That is why Neptune moves slowly and Mercury races around the Sun.

Why do planets not fall into the Sun? They do fall, but they are also moving sideways very fast. The planet keeps missing the Sun, and that endless falling-and-missing is an orbit.

The simulation adds up the pull from every body on every other body, moves everything forward by one hour, and repeats. It uses the leapfrog method, which keeps energy almost constant. The "energy drift" in the top-left corner shows how accurate it is. A tiny number means the physics is behaving.

Masses, radii and orbit sizes are rounded values from NASA's planetary fact sheets. Planet starting positions are arbitrary, not today's real positions. Orbits are drawn in 2D.
